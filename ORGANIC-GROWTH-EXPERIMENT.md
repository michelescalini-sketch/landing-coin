# Landing Coin Organic Growth Experiment

**Status:** ONGOING

Landing Coin (LANDING) is a public experiment in what happens when a crypto project refuses to manufacture traction.

The experiment asks a simple question:

> Can a small Solana token earn genuine discovery, credibility and community interest without pretending to be bigger than it is?

LANDING does not claim that organic growth will succeed. The purpose of this document is to record what is tried, what happens, what fails, and what can be learned from the process.

---

## The Hypothesis

A crypto project should be able to build visibility and credibility without artificially inflating its activity.

LANDING tests whether transparent technical foundations, selective outreach, useful content and genuine community participation can produce meaningful organic signals over time.

The experiment is deliberately allowed to fail.

---

## What We Refuse to Do

LANDING does not use:

- wash trading;
- artificial trading volume;
- fake holders;
- bots designed to simulate activity;
- artificial engagement;
- paid shilling presented as organic interest;
- manufactured social proof;
- fake partnerships;
- promotional token swaps;
- engagement-driven giveaways designed primarily to inflate metrics;
- promises of price appreciation or financial returns.

Poor-looking metrics are preferable to fake ones.

---

## What We Do Instead

The project focuses on:

- technical transparency;
- written and asynchronous communication;
- selective editorial outreach;
- participation in relevant Solana communities;
- genuine project and builder relationships;
- educational and editorial content;
- free ecosystem opportunities when they provide real value;
- organic discovery;
- public documentation of successes and failures.

Activity is not performed simply to create the appearance of momentum.

## Signals We Track

The experiment is interested in signals that are difficult to manufacture without creating real interest.

Examples include:

- unsolicited replies or questions;
- organic mentions;
- meaningful community discussion;
- editorial acceptance;
- inbound collaboration requests;
- unpaid listings or features;
- repeat visitors or readers;
- genuine on-chain activity;
- people voluntarily contributing feedback, content or expertise.

No individual metric is treated as proof of success.

---

## Failures and Rejections

Failures are part of the experiment and are not removed from the record.

Examples may include:

- rejected listing applications;
- unanswered outreach;
- community channels that produce no useful interaction;
- content that receives little attention;
- platforms abandoned because the expected value is too low;
- initiatives that consume more time than the benefit they produce.

A failed action can still produce useful information.

---

## Current Findings

These findings are provisional.

### 1. Transparency does not create traction by itself

Disabling token authorities and publishing clear project information improves verifiability, but it does not automatically create an audience.

### 2. Honest early metrics can look unattractive

A project that refuses to manufacture volume or engagement may initially appear inactive when judged by conventional crypto dashboards.

That appearance may be accurate.

### 3. Not every distribution channel deserves attention

A platform can be legitimate and still provide little practical value to a very small project.

Opportunity cost matters.

### 4. Editorial ideas can travel further than token promotion

The broader question behind LANDING — whether crypto projects can grow without fake traction — is potentially more interesting than another promotional message about a token.

### 5. The experiment needs time

Organic traction cannot be meaningfully evaluated after only a few days.

The absence of immediate growth is data, not necessarily failure.

---

## What Would Count as Progress?

Progress does not require a specific token price.

Examples of meaningful progress would include:

- independent discussion of LANDING;
- unsolicited community participation;
- credible editorial coverage;
- ecosystem recognition;
- organic trading activity;
- useful collaborations;
- people discovering the project without direct solicitation.

Conversely, large numbers produced primarily through paid or artificial activity would not count as evidence for the experiment.
